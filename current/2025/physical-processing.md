Once we had worked through the intellectual order, the actual physical processing came quite naturally. It took around one to two weeks and involved a lot of repetitive manual labor. The typical rust and dust of archival work, if you will. We spent many hours replacing crummy staples with archival paperclips, arranging materials in chronological order, and updating file labels. A majority of the collection was rehoused into acid-free archival boxes and folders for long term preservation. During physical processing, it became very apparent that the collection had only been roughly processed, likely following the archival mantra of “More Product, Less Process.” While arrangement was logical at the series-level, clarity wavered at the file-level and was lost at the item-level. Given the massive backlog most institutional archives must reckon with, this efficiency-focused approach is understandable, but it also reveals an unfortunate carelessness for the preservation of Asian history at Bryn Mawr.

“Series 2: Financial Records” underwent the most drastic physical changes. As mentioned in our entry on collection assessment, as a part of the series, hundreds of index-sized donor cards had been shoved into a Hollinger box and grouped together using rubber bands. With age, the rubber bands began to decay and embrittled itself on the donor cards, which stained the materials. The cards were rehoused into a smaller box with suitable dimensions, sorted by DIY mini folders that required some arts and crafts. A rolodex of alumnae/i donor information and the committee letterhead stamp were also stuffed beside the donor cards. Both items were rehoused into Box 7, a petite, acid-free container from the Arts and Artifacts department that we padded with paper. 

“Series 4: Scholar Files” was relocated to a Bankers box to accommodate for the large amount of new folders, and some of the “Series 2: Financial Records” files were also moved there. The scholar files themselves were each labeled with the scholar’s name and graduating year (or graduation status), alphabetized by their surname. The “Series 3: Admissions” files were pulled from the Admissions Office Records, relabeled appropriately, and given their own Hollinger box. The long sample exams from Tsing Hua got their own flat, legal-sized box. Meanwhile, “Series 1: Administrative Records” stayed largely the same, with minor relabeling and refiling. 

Though physical processing took quite a bit of time, it was very satisfying to see the collection tangibly organized. We tried to be as detail-oriented and streamlined as possible, making sure to update each other on any major changes we made. While it was one thing to talk about rearranging the collection, it was certainly another to actually transform the collection, one folder at a time, one box at a time.
